## The premise was half wrong, and the half that was wrong is the alarming half

07-09's own "what I did NOT do" said: *"`_dq_acknowledgements` has never run
against the real feature manifests, and it executes at step 9 — after the
look. … Wave 9 should expect this to be the line that breaks."*

Step 9 was not a line that could break. It called
`data.store.dq_acknowledgement_ids`, which is `_dq_pause_findings` with its
`unacknowledged` half **discarded**:

```python
def dq_acknowledgement_ids(manifest, *, registry_root, lake_root):
    _unacked, acks = _dq_pause_findings(...)      # ← the refusal, thrown away
    return [ack_id for ack_id, _sha in acks]
```

A `failed` day with no acknowledgement therefore did not raise at step 9. It
returned an empty list, and the run was tagged `dq_ack_ids=none` — a silent
green, not a late red.

The thing that actually refuses on DQ state is `store._enforce_dq_pause`,
called by `features.tier.load_features` as its gate 3. And
`harness.accessor.materialize` calls `load_features` over **every** upstream
feature manifest as its first act (accessor.py:132) while
`harness.budget.record_look` is its **last** (accessor.py:209). So a DQ pause
has always failed before a look was recorded. Measured, not argued — see
mutation 1 below, where the check was deleted and every one of the eight look
counters stayed at 0.

**What the gap really was:** the pause verdict on data committed before the
invocation began was reached only after `materialize` had hash-verified every
upstream partition's bytes, and it arrived as an uncaught `DQPauseError`
traceback rather than as a named refusal beside the other five — with no
`look_report AFTER` printed, which is the line an operator reads to confirm
nothing was spent.

## What the check verifies, in plain terms

For each of the seven feature manifests the segment manifest names, and for
each date inside them: read `lake/dq/date=<d>/report.parquet`, keep only the
rows matching `(symbol, stream)` **and scoring this exact manifest id**, and
take the worst status. `ok` needs at least one `ok` row and no
`failed`/`degraded`/unknown row; a manifest with no rows of its own is
`missing`, and `missing` is not `ok`. Any non-`ok` day needs an
acknowledgement JSON at
`lake_registry/dq_acknowledgements/<symbol>__<stream>__<date>.json` that
parses, names that exact day, carries a non-future timestamp, lists **every**
finding of the day by check name and status, and is git-committed
byte-identical to `HEAD`. The ids of the acknowledgements actually relied on,
and the sha256 of the bytes the gate honoured, become the run's
`dq_ack_ids` / `dq_ack_sha256` tags.

**All of it could move, and none of it had to be duplicated.** The pre-flight
does not reimplement any of the above — `models.slice.dq_preflight` calls
`store._enforce_dq_pause`, which *is* gate 3's own function, over the same raw
manifest bodies step 9 already read. Step 9 now calls that same
`dq_preflight`. So "the pre-flight sees everything step 9 sees" is true by
construction rather than by argument, and the two cannot drift.

Two incidental improvements fell out of using the gate instead of
reconstructing its answer:

- The sha256 now comes back from the gate (the bytes it validated and proved
  committed, WR-19) instead of from a second read of a path rebuilt by
  splitting the ack id on `__`. The old reconstruction could in principle hash
  bytes the gate never honoured.
- The old `SliceError` for an ack id that did not split into three parts is
  gone, because the id is no longer parsed. It was unreachable for
  `BTCUSDT__features__<date>` and covered by no test.

## Residue left at step 9: none from the check, one from the flags

`dq_preflight` is called in full at both places, so nothing of the check
itself stayed behind. The residue is in **which flags get the pre-flight**:

| flag | pre-flight | why |
|---|---|---|
| `--select` | yes | first act is a materialize |
| `--spend-val-look` | yes | first act is a materialize |
| `--respend-val-look` | yes | first act is a materialize |
| `--freeze` | **no** | refits from the train cache; never calls `materialize` |
| `--resume-from-cache` | **no** | cache hit at steps 1 and 6; never calls `materialize` |

Neither excluded flag can spend a look, so neither can lose one to this. What
excluding them leaves open is **late, not expensive**, and the price should be
stated accurately or the user will weigh the decision against a false one: on a
full cache-hit path `load_features` never runs, so gate 3 never runs, and a day
that turned `failed` after the cache was written is first noticed by
`dq_preflight` at step 9 — which refuses. `--resume-from-cache` does not
complete such a run. It costs the same thing mutation 1 below measures: the
work done between step 7 and step 9, and a raise arriving late instead of
early. **No look is at risk** — the val cache is present by that flag's own
precondition, so the recovery is the already-documented one (commit the
untracked prediction-table manifest, re-invoke) and the re-invocation is a
cache hit.

It is still a decision rather than an oversight, because the honest options
differ: refuse in the pre-flight (which blocks the salvage path outright, on a
day whose *cached frame* was materialized while the day was still `ok`), or
let step 9 keep it and record the waiver. **Not decided here, and not silently
patched. Flagged for the user.** The same applies to `--freeze`, which would
write a frozen body resting on a since-paused day.

## Proof the pre-flight spends nothing

Three independent measurements, all read before the first commit and again
after the last:

1. **Structural.** `dq_preflight` reads committed manifest JSON, DQ report
   Parquet, acknowledgement files and `git`. It imports no MLflow, calls no
   `materialize`, and calls no `record_look`. `record_look` is reachable only
   through `harness.accessor.materialize`, which the slice reaches only through
   `models.cache.materialize_once`.

2. **Counted.** `test_the_cli_refuses_a_paused_day_before_any_materialize`
   wraps `models.slice.materialize_once` in a counter and asserts
   `calls == []` on both `--select` and `--spend-val-look` when the pre-flight
   refuses. Zero calls, so nothing downstream of it ran at all.

3. **On the real store.** The dry run above read all eight counters of the
   approved manifest immediately before and immediately after calling
   `dq_preflight` against the real lake: `{train: 0, val: 0, held_out: 0,
   oof_block_0..4: 0}` both times, `UNCHANGED: True`.

## The refusal, quoted

```
FAIL: dq_preflight: upstream feature manifest 31f24089cb59398f4c7c85e0613fcc4bf3960b9d24402e2440127a852145c453 of BTCUSDT.features (date(s) 2026-09-13) is DQ-paused -- DQ pause: BTCUSDT.features has unacknowledged day(s): 2026-09-13: failed (findings fixture_features=failed; no acknowledgement file). Add a valid, git-committed acknowledgement JSON (date, symbol, stream, reason, who, when, acknowledged; e.g. .../registry/dq_acknowledgements/BTCUSDT__features__2026-09-13.json) to proceed. Until that is committed this slice cannot read the day, so no look is worth spending on it.
```

It names the manifest, the date, the findings by check name, the exact path to
write, and the fields that path's file must carry. The remedy sentence is
forwarded from `store._enforce_dq_pause` rather than reworded, so the two can
never say different things.

**The anti-vacuity counterparts**, both asserted:

- `test_a_committed_acknowledgement_makes_the_paused_day_pass_and_is_reported`
  — the same `failed` day passes once a valid, git-committed acknowledgement
  covering `fixture_features=failed` exists, and comes back as
  `["BTCUSDT__features__2026-09-13"]` with a 64-hex sha matching the file's
  own bytes.
- `test_preflight_is_green_and_ackless_when_every_upstream_day_is_ok` — two
  `ok` days return `([], [])`. An `ok` day waives nothing, so the tag is
  *empty*, not merely *present*.
- `test_an_uncommitted_acknowledgement_does_not_satisfy_the_preflight` — a
  valid but uncommitted ack still refuses, so the pre-flight cannot pass on a
  file `load_features` would later reject.

### Mutation 1 — delete the pre-flight call from the CLI

`if args.select or args.spend_val_look or args.respend_val_look:` →
`if False:`

Hashes: `68a72046…` → `99531b31…` → `68a72046…`.

*Observable predicted first:* three failures. `[--select]` fails not on an

*Consequence measured separately, not glossed.* The `--select` branch prints

## What was NOT done

- **`--freeze` and `--resume-from-cache` were left unguarded**, as scoped. On a
  full cache-hit path nothing calls `load_features`, so a day that turns
  `failed` after the cache was written is caught only at step 9 — late, in the
  one path where the look is already paid for. Named above as a decision for
  the user, deliberately not patched.
- **No non-`ok` real day exists**, so `_dq_verdict_for_date` was never
  exercised against real `failed`/`degraded`/`missing` lake data. Every
  refusal in this work fired against synthetic `tmp_path` reports. The dry run
  proves the happy path on real data and nothing more.
- **`mvp/data/dq/report.py` and `mvp/data/dq/feature_checks.py` were not
  read.** The DQ *semantics* were taken from the consumer side
  (`store._dq_verdict_for_date`, `_dq_pause_findings`,
  `validate_dq_acknowledgement`) because that is the code the pre-flight
  calls. If the producer writes a shape the consumer misreads, nothing here
  would notice.
- **`STATE.md` and `ROADMAP.md` were not updated, and nothing was pushed.**
  This is a gap-closure task inside plan 07-09, not a plan of its own: it
  advances no plan counter and completes no requirement. Two commits sit on
  `feature/phase-07-regression-track-vertical-slice` locally.
- **No look was spent and no MLflow run was created anywhere in this work.**
  No `materialize` against the real lake, no write to
  `/Volumes/ProjectsSSD/aihedgefund/mlflow`, no capture daemon started.
- **The acknowledgement-sha-conflict refusal** inside `dq_preflight` (the same
  ack hashing differently for two upstream manifests) is unreachable without a
  mid-run edit of the file and is covered by no test. It is three lines
  standing in for a race, and it is argued rather than proven — the same
  category of claim this document spent its first section deflating.
