<interfaces>
From `mvp/data/time_ns.py` (import, do not redefine):
```
QTY_SCALE: int = 100_000_000   # the project's one 1e-8 fixed-point scale, already used for quantities
```
Research Q2's recommendation is to reuse this exact scale for prices too, rather than invent a
second fixed-point convention: `PRICE_SCALE = QTY_SCALE` (an alias import, documented as such).

Research Q1 (measured 2026-09-23, `curated/symbol=BTCUSDT/stream={bookTicker,trade}/date=2026-09-13`):
tick = 0.1 USDT exactly (scaled gcd 10,000,000 of 100,000,000 units/USDT); lot step = 0.001 BTC
exactly (scaled gcd 100,000 of 100,000,000 units/BTC), agreeing across `trade.qty`, `bid_qty`,
`ask_qty`. Median mid on that day: $77,061.35 -> one lot ($77.06 notional), nonzero.

Research Q2 (measured on 34,334,580 real prices): `round(price * PRICE_SCALE) // TICK_SIZE_SCALED`
disagrees with `round(price/0.1)` and `round(price*10)` on ZERO rows; zero rows sit within `1e-8`
of a tie boundary. This is the recommended conversion — matches the existing fixed-point idiom,
no accuracy tradeoff versus the alternatives.

Critical Finding 2: `floor(100 / price / 0.001) * 0.001` is exactly zero for any `price > 100_000`
USDT; exactly `0.001` (one lot) at `price == 100_000` and below, down to the smallest price this
formula is ever evaluated at in this project's real data (median $77,061.35 -> one lot, $77.06).
